How the entropy layer is calculated

The entropy background is a degree-64 truncated kneading-determinant estimate on the same 6000 × 6000 parameter grid as the Lyapunov background. These are different quantities: a periodic attracting orbit can have a negative Lyapunov exponent while the map still has positive topological entropy.

Map and domain

fu,v(x) = ½(u − v)(4x³ − 3x) + ½(u + v)

The two turning points are −½ and +½. The entropy calculation uses the continuous extension of this cubic to the compactified real line [−∞,+∞], which is homeomorphic to a compact interval. This convention includes nonescaping repellers when the critical orbits escape. The points at infinity are fixed when u > v and form a two-cycle when u < v. Constant maps on u = v are assigned entropy zero.

Once an orbit crosses the same proven escape radius used for the Lyapunov scan, its future lap sequence is continued symbolically: it remains in an outer lap for positive leading coefficient and alternates outer laps for negative leading coefficient. This avoids floating-point overflow without discarding the repeller's symbolic information.

Finite kneading determinants

Both one-sided germs are accounted for at each turning point. At a critical-point hit, the propagated germ direction selects the lap; no arbitrary numerical offset is added. The lap orientations are (+,−,+) for u > v and (−,+,−) for u < v.

The kneading matrix K(t) has two rows and three lap columns. Its entries are the signed itinerary-series differences between the right and left germs. The constant rows are (−1,1,0) and (0,−1,1). For this continuous cubic, the two base-point orbits at a turning point coalesce after their first image, so each later row coefficient has one nonzero entry, equal to +2 or −2.

D(t) = det K(1)(t) / (1 − ε1t),   DN(t) = ∑n=0N dntn

K(1) deletes the first lap column, and ε1 is that lap's orientation. Multiplication and formal-series division are truncated consistently through degree N. The displayed estimate is −log r64, where r64 is the smallest positive real zero of D64 in (0,1). If no such root is found, the finite-order estimate is zero. This does not certify that the true entropy vanishes.

Root search uses Bernstein sign variations to subdivide intervals, followed by bracketed refinement. Ambiguous nearly coincident roots are sent to Arb high-precision root isolation. No estimate is substituted from a Lyapunov exponent.

Each distinct matrix is stored as an exact packed coefficient key. Equal matrices share a determinant; equal determinants share one cached entropy result at each truncation order. The saved matrix-ID grid maps these results back to parameter space. Rendering and switching backgrounds do not repeat root calculations.

The matrix construction was checked against Kneading.jl, including exact critical-point hits and both orientation patterns. The GPU keys matched the reference keys on all 11 test maps. Known examples recover zero and log 3 as expected. Independent Arb isolation agreed on 212 test matrices at each order, and on 300 sampled production determinants at each order; maximum final entropy differences were below 5 × 10−14. There were 167 high-precision fallbacks at degree 64 and 10 at degree 32.

Reading the entropy background

Black represents the zero finite-order estimate. Gold increases toward log 3, the three-lap entropy bound. Gray marks the 164,130 parameter points (about 0.46% of the grid) where |h64 − h32| > 0.01. In Lyapunov mode, gray instead denotes an escaping critical orbit.

The degree comparison is a truncation-sensitivity diagnostic, not a rigorous error bound. Agreement between orders does not prove convergence, and floating-point itinerary errors can matter close to critical relations. All raw degree-32 and degree-64 estimates are retained in the research output, including cells displayed in gray. The image does not use the bounded-coefficient ansatz from the error-estimation article; multimodal determinant coefficients need not be uniformly bounded.

Finding a chaotic orbit

The explorer's Find chaotic orbit button uses the two critical-value kneading sequences and the lap orientations at the exact selected parameters. The search for an itinerary is symbolic: it does not build a spatial preimage partition, require expanding interval derivative bounds, read the entropy image, or repeat the GPU sweep. Numerical inverse branches are used afterward to recover coordinates for plotting.

The alphabet is L, M, R, ordered by position, with orientations (+,−,+) when u > v and (−,+,−) when u < v. A candidate tail is compared to a critical-value itinerary in signed lexicographic order: at the first differing symbol, the order is reversed if the product of the preceding lap orientations is negative. Each symbol imposes the bounds corresponding to that lap's image. For u > v, L requires the following tail to lie below the itinerary of u; M requires it to lie between those of v and u; R requires it to lie above that of v. The inequalities reverse appropriately for u < v. Exact critical-point hits have separate ordered symbols between the open laps. Escaped critical orbits are continued symbolically in the outer laps.

A finite automaton tracks all still-undecided comparisons against prefixes of the upper and lower kneading sequences. A transition is rejected as soon as it violates a bound. If a comparison matches the entire available prefix, it is also rejected rather than assumed admissible. Thus each graph describes a strict inner symbolic approximation, conditional on the numerically computed kneading prefixes. Prefix lengths 16, 32, 64, 128, and 256 are tried successively. This avoids inferring an infinite periodic kneading tail from a finite numerical observation.

The search finds recurrent components with two differently labeled outgoing edges that both return to the same state. The resulting two return words begin with different symbols, so their concatenations are unambiguously coded. Successive binary integers, concatenated together, choose between these words; the infinite choice sequence contains every finite binary word and is not eventually periodic. Each complete return resets the automaton state, so all concatenations satisfy the same kneading restrictions. A positive-entropy branching subsystem is required; a nonperiodic sequence alone is not used as a test for chaos.

The first 104,097 orbit coordinates are reconstructed by pulling back numerical cylinder intervals through the cubic's specified monotone branches, with at least 4096 future symbols as a buffer. Reconstruction checks the prescribed lap at every stored iterate, cylinder widths, and the one-step residual |f(xn) − xn+1|. It also requires a positive average log absolute derivative in every complete 4096-step block. The display reports the 4096-step average starting at the selected discard count. Coordinate intervals use ordinary floating-point bisection, not outward-rounded interval arithmetic; these checks are numerical validation, not a rigorous certificate.

A repelling orbit generally cannot be followed indefinitely by forward iteration of a rounded initial value: rounding may send it into a periodic basin or to infinity. The explorer therefore retains the full backward-reconstructed trace. Clicking the cobweb switches back to forward iteration. Changing parameters invalidates the found trace. CSV export labels the method as kneading_symbolic_inverse and includes each iterate's lap symbol.

The work budget is 8192 states per graph, 128 branching candidates, return words of at most 512 symbols, and numerical reconstruction of up to 12 candidates per prefix length. A failed or unresolved search remains inconclusive about the entropy. Regression tests include u = 0.79173279, v = −0.65347389, where both critical orbits approach an attracting period-six cycle while the symbolic finder recovers a repelling chaotic orbit. They also cover escaping critical orbits, both leading-coefficient signs, exact critical hits, and the maximum supported discard count.
